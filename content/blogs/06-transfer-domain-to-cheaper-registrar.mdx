Domain renewals are sneaky. You buy a domain at a great introductory price, forget about it for a year, and then get hit with a renewal invoice that's 2-3x what you originally paid. The good news — you can transfer your domain to a cheaper registrar and keep everything exactly as it is. Same domain name, no downtime, no SEO impact.

## What is a domain transfer?

A domain transfer moves the _registration_ of your domain from one company to another. Your domain name, website, email, and DNS records stay completely untouched. Think of it like switching your phone to a new carrier but keeping your number.

## Before you start

Check these things before initiating anything:

- **Domain age** — ICANN requires a domain to be at least 60 days old before it can be transferred.
- **Recent transfer** — If your domain was transferred in the last 60 days, you'll need to wait.
- **Time remaining** — Start at least 2 weeks before expiry. Transfers take 5–7 days and you don't want to cut it close. Most registrars add 1 year to your expiry on transfer, so you won't lose time you already paid for.
- **Renewal price at the new registrar** — Check the _renewal_ price, not just the transfer price. Some registrars offer cheap transfers but expensive renewals.

## Step 1 — Unlock your domain

Domains are locked by default to prevent unauthorized transfers. Log into your current registrar's dashboard and find the domain management page. Look for a setting called **Transfer Lock**, **Domain Lock**, or **Registrar Lock** and turn it off.

## Step 2 — Get your EPP / Authorization code

This is a one-time code that proves you own the domain and authorizes the transfer. In your current registrar's domain settings, look for **EPP Code**, **Authorization Code**, **Transfer Key**, or **Auth Code** — different registrars use different names, but it's the same thing.

Most registrars will either display it immediately or email it to your registered address. Copy it and keep it handy.

## Step 3 — Initiate the transfer at your new registrar

Go to your new registrar's website and find their domain transfer page — usually under **Domains → Transfer a Domain**. Enter your domain name, complete checkout, and paste in your EPP code when prompted.

## Step 4 — Approve the transfer

After initiating the transfer, your current registrar sends an approval email to your registered address. Open it and click **Approve** as soon as you see it — approving manually can speed the transfer from 5–7 days down to just a few hours. If you don't see it, check spam.

## Step 5 — Wait for it to complete

The transfer processes in the background. You'll get a confirmation email from your new registrar once it's done. **Your website stays live the entire time** — DNS continues working normally during a transfer and visitors won't notice anything.

## Step 6 — Verify your nameservers

Once the transfer is complete, log into your new registrar and check that your nameservers are still set correctly under **DNS → Nameservers**. They should match exactly what you had before — whether that's Cloudflare, your hosting provider, or custom nameservers.

If they've been reset to the new registrar's defaults, update them back to what they were. Everything else — hosting, email, SSL certificates — is unaffected as long as nameservers are correct.

<Callout type="info" title="Using Cloudflare for DNS?">
  If Cloudflare manages your DNS, the transfer has zero effect on your setup.
  Your nameservers point to Cloudflare regardless of which registrar holds the
  registration. Just verify the Cloudflare nameservers are still set at your new
  registrar after the transfer and you're done.
</Callout>

## Troubleshooting

**Transfer is stuck or taking too long** — Check your email for an approval request from your current registrar. The transfer won't proceed until you approve it.

**EPP code is invalid** — Make sure you copied it with no extra spaces. If it was emailed to you, copy from the plain-text version rather than the HTML version.

**Current registrar won't provide the EPP code** — This is rare but it happens. Check their support docs or contact them directly — they're legally required to provide it.

**Domain expired before the transfer completed** — Contact your new registrar's support immediately. Most can still complete the transfer within a grace period after expiry, but act fast.
